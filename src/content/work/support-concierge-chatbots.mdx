## The problem

A US youth-sports organization needed two assistants. A **support bot** answers questions about schedules, rules and logistics, often personal ("when does my team play?"). A **concierge bot** helps visiting families with dining, lodging and attractions.

## Constraints

- Answers must stay current as information changes.
- The support bot needs the user's own details, not just general information.
- Out-of-scope questions must be declined cleanly.

## How it works

<ProjectDiagram slug="support-concierge-chatbots" index={0} />

The support bot searches a question-and-answer knowledge base and combines the best passages with the chat history and the user's own team and schedule. When staff mark an answer wrong, their correction is written back into the knowledge base and the index is rebuilt.

<ProjectDiagram slug="support-concierge-chatbots" index={1} />

The concierge started as a full RAG pipeline: scraping, change detection, a versioned index and a daily scheduler. Now all ~50 FAQs sit in one cached system prompt, one model call returns the route, the answer and a follow-up suggestion, and out-of-scope replies are fixed, pre-written text.

## Key decisions

1. **Chunk on meaning, not size.** The knowledge base is written as questions and answers, so chunks follow those boundaries. Oversized answers are split by sentence, with overlap.
2. **Close the loop.** Staff corrections flow back into the knowledge base, so the bot stops repeating a mistake once it's been corrected.
3. **Know when not to use RAG.** The concierge content turned out to be only about 50 FAQs, which fits in one prompt. Removing retrieval meant fewer moving parts and no retrieval misses, and prompt caching keeps it cheap.
4. **Constrain the model where it matters.** Follow-up suggestions can only come from the real FAQ list, and out-of-scope replies are fixed text, so the bot never invents a promise on the organization's behalf.

## Results

- ~60% fewer support escalations.
- A simpler concierge with one model call per message and no index to maintain.
