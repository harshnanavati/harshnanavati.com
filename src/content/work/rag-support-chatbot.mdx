## The problem

A US youth-sports organization gets the same questions from families all season: schedules, rules and logistics, and often personal ones like "when does my team play?". Staff were answering them one by one.

## Constraints

- Answers must stay current as information changes during the season.
- Many questions need the person's own team and schedule, not just general information.
- When staff correct an answer, the next person who asks must get the corrected version.

## How it works

<ProjectDiagram slug="rag-support-chatbot" index={0} />

The bot searches a question-and-answer knowledge base and combines the best passages with the chat history and the person's own team and schedule, then writes the reply. When staff mark an answer as wrong, their correction is written back into the knowledge base and the index is rebuilt, so the next family gets the corrected answer.

## Key decisions

1. **Chunk on meaning, not size.** The knowledge base is written as questions and answers, so chunks follow those boundaries. Oversized answers are split by sentence, with overlap.
2. **Close the loop.** Staff corrections are written back into the knowledge base, so the next person who asks gets the corrected answer.
3. **Personal answers from real data.** Questions about "my team" are answered from the person's own team and schedule, not guessed from general text.
4. **Measure what happens in production.** Every question is logged, which is where the numbers on this page come from.

## Results

From production logs, May 2025 to October 2026:

- 30,000+ questions handled for 8,800+ people, across 20,000+ chat sessions.
- ~86% of questions answered without falling back to the "I can't help with that" reply.
- An estimated ~60% fewer questions escalated to staff after launch.

## What I'd do next

- Build an evaluation set that checks answers for correctness, not just coverage.
- Track which corrections fix the most repeat questions, and surface those to staff first.
